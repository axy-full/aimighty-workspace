# Sound mix and timeline transport

Updated 19 September 2026 (generation controls, then Change voice and Dub in C2); mix and transport text from 14 September. The production's private edit now saves up to 64 timed audio clips bound to existing uploaded or generated audio/video assets. Each clip records its dialogue/music/SFX lane, timeline start, source in, duration, gain, stereo pan, fades, mute and solo state. Timing is in integer frames at the sequence's 24, 25 or 30 fps. Frame-rate changes retime audio positions and fades with the shots. Original video audio has a separate include control. Solo auditions the chosen clips and mutes original video audio; mute wins over solo.

Preparing a mix loads authenticated, same-origin source bytes and decodes them on the user's device. It never submits a generation or uploads sound to a mixing vendor. The timeline's Play/pause/scrub controls own preview transport. Video previews seek their actual source in points; the source player is muted because the prepared mix supplies sound. Browser transport correction bounds audible drift; it is not a sample-accurate external synchronization protocol. Changing the edit invalidates the prepared mix and downloads until rebuilt. Switching productions or leaving Edit stops playback and cancels pending media work.

The same mixer renders the movie soundtrack and exports stereo 48 kHz WAV in 24-bit integer PCM or 32-bit float. Source offsets and linear fades are scheduled on an OfflineAudioContext; centered sources preserve their original level; noncentered sources use the browser's equal-power pan law. To prevent integer and movie clipping, a mix above full scale is attenuated as a whole, and the exact reduction is shown. Float WAV describes the float container; it does not recover precision lost in compressed source media. It includes a sample-count fact chunk. No mastering EQ, dynamics, loudness compliance, surround, stems or timecode metadata are implied.

Bounds: three-minute sequence, 200 MB combined source bytes, twelve minutes of combined decoded source intervals including overlaps, 64 audio clips. Source and sequence overruns are refused for explicit clips. Older scratch soundtracks keep their original behavior (start at zero and pad a short source with silence) until edited. All audio assets and their source/reference lineage are included in the editorial package; the mix settings remain in production.json. CMX3600 stays a V1 straight-cut list. Export the WAV separately for an aligned stereo handoff into an NLE.

Verification uses synthetic audio/video and local mock accounts, with no paid provider calls: real scoped upload/save/reload, mobile controls at all five required viewport sizes, WAV header/format/frame-length assertions, sample-level checks of offsets, pan and fades, stale-output invalidation, source-overrun refusal, and a decoded movie/WAV comparison. Codec output, cancellation, account-bound export snapshots and server validation retain their regression coverage.

## Generating into the lanes (19 September 2026)

The Edit stage's sound area (the **Sound** tab on the phone, the panel under the timeline on the desktop; the shot/color/versions inspector tabs are unchanged) generates straight onto the timeline. Three doors, one panel: **Voice-over** (text-to-speech; voice picker from `/api/audio/voices` with a refresh, model choice from the account's speech models) lands on the dialogue lane, **Sound effect** (0.5–30 s, prompt influence 0–1) on the SFX lane, **Music** (10 s–5 min, instrumental switch) on the music lane. The lane can be changed before generating. No provider is named in the panel.

Every priced button quotes first: the panel asks `/api/audio` with `quoteOnly` for the exact request it will send and shows the credits on the button; the submission carries `maxCredits` and is refused if the estimate moved. Submissions reuse the node generation pattern: the request is claimed in local storage under the lane node before it is sent, sent with an idempotency key, and offered as "Recover submitted …" on reload until the server confirms a job. Each task has one Rig node (`role: sound-lane:<task>`, type `audio`, created on first use and mapped to a production shot the way a generate node is), so a generated track is a take of that node (Voice-over · v1, v2 …), appears in Activity and the library, and is filed against the production like every other track.

Placement: the playhead frame and the chosen lane are remembered per queued job (local storage, per scope and project). When the production job feed reports the job succeeded and the recovery has added its asset, the panel reads the file's own length from its header (8 s budget) and falls back to the requested length (voice-over: about 15 characters a second) to add an `audioClips` entry — start at the remembered frame, source in 0, unity gain, centre pan, no fades — then saves. The 64-clip cap and `validateAudio` still hold: a full edit refuses the placement and says so, and a failed or cancelled job is reported without a clip. A clip past the sequence end shows the existing "Clip exceeds the edit" note.

Also on the admission, not yet in the panel: **Dialogue** (`task: "dialogue"`, `lines: [{ text, voiceId }]`, model `eleven_v3`, at most 2,000 characters and 10 voices per request, billed per character like a line; `POST /v1/text-to-dialogue`). Voice change and dubbing followed in C2, below.

## Change voice and Dub (PR C2, 19 September 2026)

Two more segments in the same panel, both working on a **stored original** rather than text, both priced **per started minute of the source** (whole minutes, rounded up), and therefore both dependent on the source's length being known.

**Stored durations.** Every audio and video original the app keeps now records its length in seconds on its row — `uploads.duration_s` (existing) and `generations.duration_s` (new) — and the project asset carries it as `seconds`. It is read at store time when it can be: the upload finish route identifies audio headers (`lib/audioMeta.ts`: WAV, MP3/ID3, ADTS AAC, M4A) and measures audio and video files with the bounded mediabunny inspector (`lib/mediaSource.server.ts`: same approach as `lib/videoMetadata.server.ts` — a read budget of 32 MB, 512 reads, a 20-second deadline, files up to 100 MB, no decoder); a sealed audio take is measured from its delivered bytes; a video whose `moov` box trails its media is measured the same way. Anything still missing is measured lazily on the first read that needs it (a quote) and written back once, so the next quote is a column read. A length that cannot be read stays null **with the reason**, and the tools refuse to quote rather than price a guessed minute.

**Change voice** — `task: "voiceChange"` on `/api/audio`, `sourceUploadId` or `sourceGenId` (a project audio original, or the audio of a dialogue clip), `voiceId`, optional `removeBackgroundNoise`. Quote: ceil(seconds ÷ 60) × $0.12 (`ELEVENLABS_RATES.voiceChange`, `lib/vendorRates.ts`) → credits through `billCredits`; the reply carries `sourceSeconds` and `minutes`. Refused (422) when the length is unknown, saying why; refused for a video, a missing source, a source over 100 MB. The render reads the source's bytes through the storage backend at render time (bounded 100 MB, never on the row) and calls `POST /v1/speech-to-speech/{voice_id}` as multipart `audio` on `eleven_multilingual_sts_v2` with only the documented fields (`model_id`, optional `voice_settings`, `seed`, `remove_background_noise`; `output_format` as the query). The output is a project audio asset named `<source> · voice changed (<voice>)` under the `sound-lane:voiceChange` node; from the panel it either **replaces** the dialogue clip it was made from in place (same start, length, gain, pan and fades — only the voice changed) or adds a new dialogue clip at the playhead. A vendor 4xx is a rejection before generation (refunded); a timeout keeps the paid claim and never retries, like every other audio task.

**Dub** — its own admission and route, `/api/audio/dub` (`lib/dubbing.ts`), because a dub is an **asynchronous project**, not a render. Body: `sourceUploadId` or `sourceGenId` (audio **or** video original), `sourceLang` (`auto` detects), `targetLang`, `mode`. Modes offered: `v1` (Standard, no watermark, **$0.50/min, the default**) and `v1-watermark` ($0.33/min); the $2.20/min v2 rate is recorded but not offered, because the request field that selects it is not among the verified facts and nothing unverified is ever sent. The quote reserves **the whole price for one target language** — the vendor charges one language up front — as ceil(seconds ÷ 60) × the mode's rate, through `reserveGenerationSpend`; the button shows the credits, and `maxCredits` guards the submission. Each project is a `dubbing_jobs` row (tenant DB) with statuses **queued → submitted → dubbing → dubbed | failed | uncertain**, plus a generation row (kind audio, model `eleven_dubbing_v1`, `params.task = "dub"`, `params.dubbingStatus` mirroring the machine) so Activity, the job feed and the library see it like any track. Progress runs through the native worker event `audio/dubbing.requested` and the ten-minute cron (`recoverDubbingJobs`, in `syncPending` next to the Astra render recovery); see `docs/native-dispatch.md`. The steps, each idempotent against the row:
- **submit** once: a permanent claim (`submit_claimed_at`) is taken before `POST /v1/dubbing` (multipart `file`, `source_lang`, `target_lang`, `num_speakers` 0, `watermark`, `mode` automatic). A 4xx before work fails the job and refunds the reservation. A lost acknowledgement (timeout, dropped connection, a claim older than ten minutes with no project id) makes the job **uncertain**: the reservation is held for review and **nothing ever submits a second time** — a lost 202 may still be a charged project.
- **poll** `GET /v1/dubbing/{id}` under a two-minute lease, at most every 45 seconds per job; a transport error keeps the id and the reservation for the next pass.
- **collect**: on `dubbed`, `GET /v1/dubbing/{id}/audio/{target}` (bounded 100 MB) is stored as the generation's audio original, measured, and the job is settled **at the reserved flat rate** (no vendor cost receipt exists; the rate and minutes used are recorded on the row). A vendor `failed` after submission ends the job as failed with the reservation kept pending reconciliation: no receipt says whether the up-front charge came back.
The stored track is named `<source> · dubbed (<Language>)`; the panel shows a progress row ("submitted", "dubbing…", "outcome unconfirmed — under review") and places the result on the dialogue lane at the remembered playhead when the feed reports it succeeded, the length taken from the stored duration.

Limits: sources up to 100 MB; audio inspection budget 32 MB read / 20 s; video inspection as before (200 MB, five minutes); dubbed downloads up to 100 MB; a dub holds a workspace job slot while it runs. No paid ElevenLabs call was made for C2: the multipart shapes, prices and status words come from the official documentation of 19 September 2026, and the first live voice change and dub are each a qualification under a stated ceiling.
