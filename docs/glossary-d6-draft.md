# Particl glossary — D6 draft (21 Oct 2026)

One list of film words, one line each, in plain words. Feeds the dotted underlines in Agent mode (hover on desktop, tap on phone) and the Director pickers' tooltips. Terms chosen against the standard reference vocabulary (the kind The Filmmaker's Handbook covers); every definition here is written fresh in Particl's words — nothing is quoted from any book. No engine or vendor names anywhere.

Marks: A = can appear in Agent mode as an underlined word · D = Director mode only (pickers, Inspector). Thumbnails: every entry gets a picture or a one-second loop (D4/D6).

The owner edits this draft; then it ships as data (SOW amendment of 21 October 2026, D6).

## 1 · Story and script

* beat (A) — one moment of the story, usually a few seconds: something happens or someone decides.
* logline (A) — the whole film in one sentence.
* treatment (A) — the short document that sells the film before it is made: idea, story, look, who, where.
* script (A) — the film written down: what we see and what is said, in order.
* scene (A) — everything that happens in one place at one time.
* sequence (A) — a run of scenes that tell one part of the story.
* act (A) — a big chunk of the story: the setup, the middle, the end.
* cold open (A) — starting in the action before any title or setup.
* hook (A) — the first seconds, made to stop someone scrolling.
* tagline (A) — the short line that sells the film or the product.
* dialogue (A) — words people say on screen.
* voice-over (A) — a voice we hear that is not speaking on screen.
* monologue (A) — one person talking for a while, alone.
* montage (A) — many short shots cut together to cover time or a feeling.
* hero beat / hero shot (A) — the one moment people remember; it gets the most care.
* genre (A) — the kind of film: comedy, thriller, drama, romance, horror, action, documentary, musical, fantasy, animation.
* tone (A) — how the film feels: quiet, bold, warm, tense.
* pacing (A) — how fast the story moves and how fast it cuts.
* call to action (A) — the line at the end that tells the viewer what to do next.

## 2 · Shot sizes (how much of the person or thing is in frame)

* extreme close-up (A) — only a detail: an eye, a hand, the switch.
* close-up (A) — the face fills the frame.
* medium close-up (A) — head and shoulders.
* medium shot (A) — waist up.
* medium wide (A) — knees up.
* wide shot (A) — the whole person and the place around them.
* extreme wide (A) — the place is the subject; the person is small in it.
* macro (D) — very close on something small, so it looks big.
* two-shot (A) — two people in one frame.
* over the shoulder (A) — looking past one person's shoulder at the other.
* insert (D) — a close shot of a detail cut in to show it clearly.
* cutaway (D) — a shot of something else, used to bridge two shots.
* establishing shot (A) — a wide shot that shows where we are before we go closer.
* master shot (D) — one wide shot that covers the whole scene from start to end.
* point of view (A) — what a character sees, through their eyes.
* reaction shot (A) — the face of someone responding to what just happened.
* reverse (A) — the matching shot from the other side of a conversation.

## 3 · Angles (where the camera looks from)

* eye level (A) — the camera at the person's eye height; neutral.
* low angle (A) — the camera looks up; the subject feels bigger, stronger.
* high angle (A) — the camera looks down; the subject feels smaller.
* overhead / top shot (A) — straight down from above.
* bird's eye (D) — very high and straight down, like a map.
* tilted / Dutch angle (A) — the camera leans so the horizon slopes; unease.
* worm's eye (D) — from the ground, looking up.
* profile (A) — from the side.
* three-quarter (D) — between front and side.

## 4 · Camera moves

* static / locked off (A) — the camera does not move.
* handheld (A) — the camera is held, so it breathes and shakes a little.
* pan (A) — the camera turns left or right from one spot.
* tilt (A) — the camera turns up or down from one spot.
* push in / dolly in (A) — the camera moves closer.
* pull out / dolly out (A) — the camera moves away.
* truck (D) — the camera moves sideways.
* pedestal / crane (D) — the camera rises or sinks.
* tracking / follow (A) — the camera moves with the person.
* orbit / arc (A) — the camera circles the subject.
* zoom (A) — the picture gets closer without the camera moving; the lens changes.
* crash zoom (D) — a very fast zoom in.
* whip pan (D) — a pan so fast the picture blurs.
* rack focus (D) — the focus moves from one thing to another in the same shot.
* gimbal / steadicam (D) — a camera held steady on a rig while walking.
* drone (A) — from the air.
* slow motion (A) — the action plays slower than real life.
* time-lapse / hyperlapse (A) — hours or a journey compressed into seconds.
* freeze frame (A) — the picture stops on one frame.
* speed ramp (D) — the speed changes inside one shot.

## 5 · Lens, camera and picture

* lens / focal length (D) — how much the camera sees and how near things look: wide, normal, long.
* wide lens (D) — sees a lot; near things look big, far things small.
* normal lens (D) — close to how the eye sees.
* long / telephoto lens (D) — sees a narrow slice; brings far things close, flattens depth.
* anamorphic (D) — a wide cinema look with oval light flares and stretched blur.
* depth of field (A) — how much of the picture, front to back, is sharp.
* shallow focus (A) — only the subject is sharp; the background is soft.
* deep focus (D) — everything is sharp, front to back.
* bokeh (D) — the soft blur of the out-of-focus parts, especially lights.
* aperture / f-stop (D) — the lens opening; wider means brighter and softer backgrounds.
* exposure (D) — how bright the picture is overall.
* frame rate (A) — how many pictures a second: 24 feels like cinema, 25 is TV here, 60 is smooth.
* shutter (D) — how long each frame gathers light; sets how motion blurs.
* grain / noise (A) — the fine texture in the picture; film-like when wanted.
* resolution (A) — how much detail the picture holds: 480p (draft), 720p, 1080p, 4K.
* aspect ratio (A) — the shape of the frame: 16:9 wide, 9:16 tall for phones, 1:1 square, 2.39:1 cinema.
* camera body (D) — the camera type; names change the texture and colour of the picture a little.
* film stock (D) — the kind of film a look imitates; sets grain and colour.
* LUT (D) — a saved colour look applied to the picture.
* upscale (A) — making a picture or clip larger and sharper after the fact.
* watermark (A) — a mark on a draft that shows it is not the final.
* first frame (A) — the still a shot starts from; the storyboard frame becomes it.
* last frame (A) — the still a shot ends on.
* keyframe (D) — a frame that fixes how things look at one moment; the rest follows.

## 6 · Light

* key light (D) — the main light on the subject.
* fill (D) — the softer light that lifts the shadows.
* back light / rim (A) — light from behind that draws an edge around the subject.
* practical (A) — a light you can see in the shot: a lamp, a sign, a candle.
* motivated light (D) — light that looks like it comes from something in the scene.
* hard light (A) — sharp shadows, like direct sun.
* soft / diffused light (A) — gentle shadows, like a cloudy day.
* golden hour (A) — the warm light just after sunrise or before sunset.
* blue hour (A) — the cool light just before sunrise or after sunset.
* high key (D) — bright, few shadows; clean and open.
* low key (D) — dark, deep shadows; moody.
* noir (A) — hard shadows, strong contrast, night.
* silhouette (A) — the subject dark against a bright background.
* neon (A) — coloured light from signs and tubes.
* volumetric / haze (A) — light you can see in the air: beams, mist.
* colour temperature (A) — how warm (orange) or cool (blue) the light is.
* tungsten (A) — warm, orange light, like old bulbs.
* daylight (A) — cool, white-blue light, like the sky.
* contrast (A) — the distance between the brightest and darkest parts.
* chiaroscuro (D) — painterly light: bright subject, deep darkness around.

## 7 · Continuity and coverage

* coverage (A) — the set of shots that lets a scene cut together: wide, close, both sides.
* the line / 180-degree rule (A) — an imaginary line between two people; keep the camera on one side so they keep facing each other on screen.
* crossing the line (A) — moving the camera to the other side, so people seem to swap places; usually a mistake.
* eyeline (A) — where a person is looking; it must point at what they are looking at.
* eyeline match (D) — the look in one shot lands on the thing in the next.
* screen direction (A) — which way someone moves across the frame; it should stay the same from shot to shot.
* shot / reverse shot (A) — one person, then the other, back and forth in a conversation.
* action and reaction (A) — something happens, then we see the face that responds.
* cut on action (D) — cutting in the middle of a movement so the join is invisible.
* match cut (D) — two shots joined by a matching shape or movement.
* jump cut (A) — a cut that skips time inside the same shot; jarring unless meant.
* continuity (A) — things staying the same from shot to shot: clothes, light, props, where people stand.
* axis (D) — the line, in one word.

## 8 · Storyboard and planning

* storyboard (A) — one drawn frame per beat: the film before it moves.
* frame / panel (A) — one drawing or still in the storyboard.
* animatic (A) — the storyboard frames played in order with rough sound; a cut before the shots exist.
* previs (D) — a rough moving version of a scene, made to plan it.
* thumbnail (D) — a tiny quick sketch of a frame.
* shot list (A) — the list of shots to make, in order.
* breakdown (A) — the script taken apart into beats, people, places and things.
* shooting order (D) — the order shots are made, which need not be story order.

## 9 · Elements

* cast (A) — the people in the film.
* character sheet / turnaround (A) — one person from several angles, so they stay the same in every shot.
* wardrobe (A) — what people wear.
* prop (A) — a thing a person uses or that matters in the shot.
* set / location / environment (A) — where the scene happens.
* product (A) — the thing the ad is about.
* likeness (A) — a real person's face or voice; needs their consent on file.
* consent / release (A) — the person's recorded permission to use their face or voice.
* reference (A) — a picture or clip that shows what you mean.
* element (A) — any person, place or thing the film holds to from shot to shot.

## 10 · Look

* look (A) — how the film feels to the eye: light, colour, grain, real or drawn.
* palette (A) — the few colours the film lives in.
* mood board (A) — a page of pictures that shows the feeling.
* style frame (D) — one finished-looking still that sets the look.
* grade / colour grade (A) — the colour and contrast applied to the whole film at the end.
* texture (A) — the surface feel of the picture: clean, grainy, soft.
* photoreal (A) — looks like it was filmed.
* stylised (A) — looks drawn, painted or designed.

## 11 · Sound

* voice-over (A) — see Story: a voice we hear but do not see.
* dialogue (A) — words said on screen.
* lip-sync (A) — the mouth matching the words; needed when someone on screen talks.
* dubbing / ADR (A) — replacing or adding spoken lines after the picture is made; another language, or a cleaner take.
* music bed / under (A) — music under the voice, quieter.
* music leading (A) — music in front; the voice fits around it.
* score (D) — music written for the film.
* cue (D) — one piece of music placed at one point.
* stem (D) — one layer of the sound on its own: voice, music, effects.
* ambience / room tone (A) — the quiet sound of a place: traffic, rain, a hum.
* sound effects (A) — the sounds of things: a click, a door, footsteps.
* foley (D) — effects made to match the picture exactly.
* mix (A) — all the sound balanced together.
* level (D) — how loud one sound is.
* sync (A) — sound and picture lining up in time.
* beat map (A) — where the strong beats of a track fall, so cuts can land on them.
* drop (A) — the moment a track hits hardest.

## 12 · Edit

* cut (A) — the film assembled from the shots; also the join between two shots.
* rough cut (A) — the first full assembly, not yet tight.
* fine cut (D) — the cut after the rough one has been tightened.
* picture lock (D) — the cut is final; only sound and colour change now.
* assembly (D) — the shots laid end to end in order, before any real cutting.
* timeline (A) — the strip where the shots sit in order.
* rhythm (A) — how the cuts feel: steady, quick, lingering.
* hold (A) — staying on a shot longer.
* trim (A) — making a shot shorter at its start or end.
* transition (A) — how one shot becomes the next.
* dissolve (A) — one shot fades into the next.
* fade (A) — the picture goes to or comes from black.
* J-cut / L-cut (D) — the sound of the next or previous shot overlapping the picture.
* captions / subtitles (A) — the words on screen for what is said.
* lower third (A) — a name or title in the bottom part of the frame.
* title card / end card (A) — a frame of text: the title, the product, the sign-off.
* super (D) — text laid over the picture.
* split screen (A) — two pictures side by side.
* end on (A) — what the last frame shows: the face, the product, the title.

## 13 · Deliver

* master (A) — the finished film at full quality, the one every version is made from.
* deliverable (A) — one version the brief asks for: a length, a shape, a language.
* version (A) — the same film in another shape, length or language.
* export (A) — making the file.
* format (A) — the kind of file: mp4 for everywhere, ProRes for editors.
* codec (D) — how the picture is compressed inside the file.
* bitrate (D) — how much data the file spends per second; higher is cleaner and bigger.
* loudness (D) — the measured volume broadcasters and platforms require.
* 4K (A) — the sharpest size; four times 1080p.
* review link (A) — a page a client opens to watch and comment, without an account.
* sign-off (A) — the person who says it is done.
* safe area (D) — the part of the frame that stays visible on every screen.
* slate (D) — the frame of text before a film that says what it is.

## 14 · Particl's own words (shown, never underlined)

* take — one try at a shot.
* draft — a quick, low-resolution take to judge before paying for the final.
* final — the full-quality take, made from a kept draft.
* keep — the mark that says "this one is good".
* sent — the step's result passed on to the next step.
* options — three alternatives to pick from; all three are made and billed, and the thinking behind them is billed.
* change something — say or point at what is wrong; one quote to fix it.
* preflight — what will be made, from what, at what price, before anything is spent.
* quote — the one price, shown before you approve.
* credit (cr) — Particl's unit; 1 cr = $0.10.
* the line — see Continuity.
