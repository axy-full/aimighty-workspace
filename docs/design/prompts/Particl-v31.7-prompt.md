RULES (never printed on screen; they replace v31.6's)
1. The user is the filmmaker. Atomik designs the workflow; the user directs. Never "Atomik produces", "directed by Atomik", "AI-powered", "made with AI", or anything about choosing the right model or agent.
2. Agent mode never names an engine, anywhere. Engine names appear only where the Director picks them: the Engine tab, Director's Make, and the "Engines you can pick in Director mode" section of "How Particl works". A result made in Agent mode never reveals how it was made, in either mode. Nothing on any screen says which engine Agent mode used, how it routes, or what anything costs us.
3. Nothing intelligent is free. Anything a model does is priced and quoted first: every generation, Options, Enhance, auditions, transcription, reading a document or a reference film, research, writing, planning, re-cuts, previews, captions. Free means no model ran: the Preflight and the quote, the person's own typing and hand edits, copies, moving, Keep, undo, hand editing on the timeline, exports of what was already made and billed. Uploading: putting a file in is free; the moment a model has to look at it, that is priced by how much it has to look at (a document: Reading by pages, scanned pages add OCR; a reference film: by the minute; a recording: transcription by the minute; photos for a likeness: the training job; a reference picture inside a take: nothing extra). So the word "free" never appears next to Import, Drop or Upload anywhere; the button says only "Upload" or "Import anything", and the price appears on the Preflight the moment Atomik is about to read something, sized by the file ("Campaign brief.docx · 14 pages · Reading · 4–9 cr"). Prices read "N cr", "N–M cr", "up to N cr" or "free", lowercase; the credit is $0.10 and hovering a price shows its dollars. The words token, markup, margin, seed, model and template never appear.
4. Agent mode asks at most three questions per step, only about what the record leaves open, each concrete, each with "Decide for me"; then the Preflight, one quote with Approve · Change · Cancel, and it runs without asking again. The questions are never a fixed card: they are derived each time from the kind of film and its genre, the step, and what the record already holds, so an ad film at Storyboard asks about the product's coverage, a microdrama at Script about the cliffhanger, and a music video at Look asks nothing when the references already settle it. Every question card in the build is a sample of that, and no two productions show the same one.
5. Director mode is fully manual: words in the bar, settings in the right panel, Create greyed until the node has an engine, Enhance · 1 cr shows the full prompt before the press and has no Undo. No lasso, brush or element selector exists in Director mode anywhere.
6. Agent ⇄ Director switches at any time from the left end of the bar; nothing is lost and nothing re-lays.
7. The card is the shot. Keep is the one mark a person makes on a card, on every step; kept things travel forward by themselves when the next step is opened (KEEP AND NEXT below). Frames and shots carry their number large, in story order.
8. Two kinds of line: a dotted line between cards is a bundle; a solid wire into a node is an input. A node accepts any number of inputs and lists every one as a row.
9. The bar is the only place to type and it is docked at the bottom on every step in both modes. Nothing floats on a canvas and nothing sits under the bar: every page lays out above it.
10. The canvas is Luma's, as built in v31.6. Edit is not a canvas: it is a page with regions, and its timeline is part of the page.
11. Nothing on screen says the work was generated. No templates, no galleries of looks, no stock elements, cast or environments.
12. The desktop is where work is made; the phone is where work is judged. Neither is the other shrunk.
